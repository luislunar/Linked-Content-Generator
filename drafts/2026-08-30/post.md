Wrong question.

A client called me three months before she submitted her GSA Schedule application. "I've done my pricing homework," she said. "I benchmarked what competitors are charging on their schedules. I'm ready."

I told her she'd answered the wrong question entirely.

She'd been focused on matching the market — not on understanding the floor she was about to lock in. GSA pricing isn't a rate card you revisit next quarter. Once negotiated, your Basis of Award price becomes the ceiling on what you can charge federal buyers, and you're obligated to match any lower commercial price you give to anyone else. That floor follows you through every task order, every modification, and every recompete.

We rebuilt her pricing from a different starting point: what is the absolute lowest rate you'd accept from a federal buyer, given everything you know about your real costs? She revised her rates upward — 12% above her original draft. Her application was approved. Nine months later, her first task order landed and she had room to negotiate, not a ceiling she'd already breached.

The rate card isn't the strategy. The floor is.
