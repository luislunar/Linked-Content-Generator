Nobody talks enough about the companies that have a GSA Schedule — but can't actually use it.

I worked with a construction services firm last year. They'd held their GSA Schedule for eighteen months. Zero federal revenue. They weren't doing anything obviously wrong — no missed renewals on their radar, no notices in their inbox. They came to us thinking they needed better marketing or a BD outreach strategy.

We ran a compliance audit first.

What we found: their Trade Agreements Act (TAA) certification had quietly lapsed. Three line items in their price list had drifted out of alignment with their commercial rates — enough for any contracting officer reviewing a bid to flag it. And an Option Period notification had been missed entirely. GSA had flagged the contract. The client just hadn't seen the notice.

None of these are rare. All three make you invisible on any solicitation before a CO even reads your name.

We corrected the TAA cert, renegotiated the pricing back into compliance, and cleared the Option Period flag. Sixty days later, they submitted their first RFQ response. It was accepted.

The Schedule doesn't do the work. Keeping it compliant is the work.

What's the maintenance item you're most likely to let slip — price list reviews, TAA recertification, or Option Period tracking? Be honest.
