Before you submit your next proposal off a GSA Schedule, run this audit first.

Most contractors don't. They submit prices, get approved, then start bidding without looking back. That's how you win fewer bids than you should — or walk into a compliance issue you didn't know existed.

Five things worth checking before your next federal response goes out:

1. **Basis of Award alignment.** Your GSA price must stay at or below what you offer your best commercial customer. Gave a commercial client a discount since your Schedule was awarded? GSA may be owed the same rate.

2. **Price Reduction Clause triggers.** This clause runs automatically after award. Any commercial discount you extend post-contract can create a mandatory rate adjustment for GSA customers. It applies retroactively. Most small businesses find out at the wrong moment.

3. **Pricelist date.** Your GSA pricelist carries an expiration signal. Contracting officers check it before they read anything else. A stale pricelist flags noncompliance before your name even registers on a bid.

4. **Unlisted service lines.** Added new offerings since your Schedule was approved? They are not on your contract. You cannot legally bid them until you file a modification. Doing so anyway is a compliance violation, not a technicality.

5. **EPA clause window.** Your contract includes an Economic Price Adjustment clause that lets you raise rates when your costs move. But there are deadlines. Miss the window and your rates lock longer than your cost structure can support.

None of these take long to check. All five can quietly disqualify you if you don't.
