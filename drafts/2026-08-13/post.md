Before your next GSA Schedule annual review catches you off guard — run this five-point check first.

Most small businesses treat their Schedule like a set-it-and-forget-it contract. It isn't.

1. **Your pricing still reflects your commercial rate.** GSA's Basis of Award ties your Schedule prices to what you charge commercial clients. If you've lowered your rates since approval, GSA can demand a retroactive price reduction. Check your Most Favored Customer pricing now.

2. **SAM.gov registration is active.** An expired SAM registration doesn't just inconvenience you — it can freeze payments and technically void your ability to receive awards. Your renewal window opens 60 days before expiration. Don't miss it.

3. **Every authorized sales rep is listed on your Schedule.** Personnel changes happen fast. If someone on your team is selling under your Schedule and isn't listed as an authorized negotiator, you're out of compliance — even if the work itself is legitimate.

4. **Sales reporting is current in the 72A system.** Your IFF (Industrial Funding Fee) filings must match actual Schedule sales. Late or missing reports are one of the top triggers for a Contractor Assessment. This happens more than most people expect.

5. **Your company info matches exactly across SAM, your pricelist, and your invoices.** Name, UEI, NAICS codes — small mismatches get flagged. I've seen firms lose awards over a DBA that wasn't updated in SAM.

The annual review isn't the hard part. The audit that comes from ignoring these five is.
