"What price should I charge for my GSA contract?"

That's the wrong question.

The right question: *What's your Basis of Award — and are you actively protecting it?*

Your Schedule price isn't a number you pick at award and forget. It's anchored to your most favorable commercial deal. Ignore that after award, and you're building a contract you can't compete with — or one the government can claw back on.

Five GSA pricing rules most holders learn the hard way:

1. **Know your BOA customer.** Your contract is priced against your best commercial deal. If a private client is getting a better rate than your federal pricing, that's your Basis of Award — and it matters.

2. **The Price Reduction Clause has teeth.** Discount below your BOA commercially post-award? The government may be entitled to that same reduction retroactively. This is not hypothetical.

3. **Your pricelist must stay current.** A stale modification means a stale pricelist. Stale pricelist means you can't respond to RFQs. End of pipeline — literally.

4. **Negotiate headroom at award.** The Contracting Officer will push your rates down during negotiation. If you don't build margin into your commercial price list before submission, you'll lock yourself into rates you can't actually compete with.

5. **Every change requires a mod.** New service line, adjusted rate, updated discount — it goes through a formal modification. No workarounds.

The Schedule isn't "set it and forget it."
