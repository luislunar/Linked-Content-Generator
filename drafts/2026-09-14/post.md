Nobody talks about sources sought notices. That's the whole problem.

By the time an RFP drops on SAM.gov, the buying decision is already half-formed. The agency has spent weeks in market research. Preferred vendors are on their radar. The solicitation is almost a formality.

Sources sought notices are published before any solicitation. They signal: this opportunity exists, here's the timeline, here's the NAICS code — and the agency wants to know who's out there. Responding to one puts you in the conversation before the competition is set.

Most small businesses search for "open solicitations." That's arriving to a race that's halfway run.

The pipeline doesn't start when the solicitation drops. It starts when the agency starts asking questions.
