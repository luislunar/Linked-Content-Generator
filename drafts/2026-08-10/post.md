SAM.gov doesn't send you a goodbye email when your registration expires. I wish it did.

My first year in GovCon consulting, I worked with a client who'd done everything right. Two years of commercial revenue. GSA Schedule approved. Good relationships with a couple of contracting officers. Then an agency issued an RFQ — the exact kind of work they'd been waiting for. We pulled up their SAM.gov profile to confirm the entity details. Registration had lapsed four months earlier. Nobody noticed. Not them. Not the CO they'd been checking in with. Just... gone.

SAM.gov gives you a one-year registration window. Renewal opens 60 days before expiration. Miss it and your entity deactivates — meaning federal agencies cannot legally award or pay through a lapsed registration. No grace period. No second shot at that RFQ. One missed renewal date froze everything they'd been building toward.

The fix has always been simple: put a 90-day reminder on your calendar before your anniversary date. Check your own entity status at sam.gov — don't wait on an email that may or may not arrive. The system has improved over the years, but it is not your compliance manager.

The Schedule is the key. SAM.gov is the door. Don't let it lock itself while you're busy running a business.
