She had a GSA Schedule for 14 months. Not a single federal award.

Her SAM.gov registration was current. Her NAICS codes were right. Her capability statement was polished. On paper, she had everything she needed.

The problem wasn't her Schedule. It was how she thought about finding work.

She was waiting for the phone to ring. In federal contracting, the phone doesn't ring until you've already been on someone's radar for months.

Three things changed that:

→ She started monitoring recompetes — active contracts in her space expiring within 18 months. That's where you find solicitations where the agency already knows what it needs and is ready to award.

→ She began responding to every Sources Sought notice in her NAICS codes. These are pre-solicitation notices agencies use to identify vendors before writing the actual solicitation. Most small businesses ignore them. That's a mistake.

→ She attended one industry day hosted by her top target agency. Didn't pitch anything. Just listened, introduced herself, and followed up by email. That single touchpoint put her on a contracting officer's list.

Eleven months later: her first federal task order.

The Schedule opens the door. You still have to walk through it.
