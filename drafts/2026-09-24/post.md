Nobody talks about this enough: A bad GSA price negotiation doesn't just hurt one deal. It shapes every deal that comes after it.

I had a client — solid IT services company, $4M in annual commercial revenue — who came to us thrilled. He'd already gone through the GSA application process on his own and had a Schedule offer in hand. But when we reviewed his proposed pricing, I had to slow him down.

His rates were 22% below what he charged his best commercial clients.

Here's why that's a problem. The GSA Schedule uses a concept called Basis of Award (BOA). It ties your approved federal price to your most-favored commercial pricing. That means every time you discount a commercial client going forward, you may be legally obligated to extend that same discount to the federal government. Price too low at the start, and you've built a ceiling — not a floor.

We helped him go back, renegotiate the rates, and rewrite his internal discount approval process. It added two months to the timeline. But it saved him from years of margin erosion down the road.

The Schedule is a long-term commitment. The paperwork is the start. Getting the pricing right is the part that protects the value of that commitment.
