Nobody talks about how much bad GSA pricing costs you — before you win a single contract.

A client came to us last year. IT consulting firm. They had gotten their GSA Schedule approved on their own — spent months on the paperwork, felt proud of it. Then sat on it for 14 months without a single award.

When we audited their contract, the problem was in plain sight: their labor rates were 18% above what two competing contractors on the same SIN were charging. Every Contracting Officer who ran a price comparison passed on them. The Schedule was technically approved — it was just economically invisible.

We went back and renegotiated their Basis of Award pricing, tightened the rate structure to stay competitive without giving the work away. Three months after repricing, first RFQ response. Five months in, first award.

The Schedule doesn't fail. The pricing does.

If you have a GSA Schedule sitting dormant, pricing is the first thing to audit — not your capability statement, not your marketing. Look at what your competitors on the same SIN are charging and get honest about where you sit.
