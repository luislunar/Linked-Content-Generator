Nobody talks enough about the difference between teaming as a subcontractor and actually building your own federal presence.

You can team your way onto federal contracts without a GSA Schedule. Primes bring in commercial subs every day. The work is real. The checks clear.

But here's what those arrangements don't build: your own contract vehicle. Your own CPARS ratings. Your own past performance record. Your own pipeline.

Every dollar you earn as a sub gets credited to the prime's history. When that teaming agreement ends — and they always end — you walk away exactly where you started, except with some war stories and a gap in your BD calendar.

Teaming is a valid tactic. It is not a strategy.

The small businesses I've seen break into federal contracting sustainably aren't choosing between teaming and getting a GSA Schedule. They're doing both. They sub to learn the market and earn near-term revenue while building their own Schedule in parallel — so when the teaming agreement runs its course, they have a vehicle, a record, and a pipeline that belongs to them.

The question isn't "should I team or get a Schedule?" It's "while I'm teaming, am I building my own foundation — or just building someone else's?"

If you've been in a teaming arrangement, what did it actually do for your long-term pipeline? Did it open doors or just keep you comfortable?
