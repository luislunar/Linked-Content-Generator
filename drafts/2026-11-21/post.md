Nobody talks enough about what happens after you get the Schedule.

A business owner called me a while back. Three years into having his GSA Schedule — never touched it once.

His company had grown. Commercial rates had gone up. Costs had changed. But his Schedule prices were still frozen at Day 1.

He submitted a quote on a federal RFQ. The contracting officer pulled his Schedule on GSA Advantage, saw rates 40% lower than what he'd quoted, and called him on it. He lost the contract.

After approval, you're responsible for filing modifications when your offerings or pricing change, responding to mass mods that require your signature, keeping annual sales reporting current, and renewing your SAM.gov registration every year. Miss any of these and you're either out of compliance or completely invisible to buyers.

The 100+ hours you spend getting approved? That's just the beginning. The businesses that actually win on Schedule treat maintenance like part of the job — not a burden they'll deal with "eventually."
