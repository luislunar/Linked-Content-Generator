Nobody talks enough about the vehicle mismatch problem in GovCon.

A small IT consulting firm came to us after spending six months — and about $18,000 in BD consultant fees — trying to "get on SEWP." Their advisor had told them SEWP was the gold standard for federal IT procurement. That part is technically true.

What the advisor missed: SEWP doesn't take open applications. You earn a spot by winning a task order under an existing holder, or through a narrow sole-source justification approved by the CO. For a company with zero federal past performance, it was the wrong door entirely.

GSA MAS (Multiple Award Schedule) was the right vehicle. It's open-application, it covers the same IT categories, and it puts you in the pool where agencies can find you directly via GSA Advantage and eBuy. No invitation required.

We got them on the Schedule in roughly seven months. They received their first task order inquiry within month ten.

The lesson isn't that SEWP is bad — it's the right fit for certain companies at a certain stage. The lesson is that the right vehicle depends on your situation: your past performance history, your SINs, which agencies buy what you sell, and how early you are in federal contracting. Picking the wrong one doesn't just cost you time. It consumes the BD budget most small firms can't afford to burn twice.

If you're new to federal contracting, start with the vehicle that lets you in the door. Optimize later.
