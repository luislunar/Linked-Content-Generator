Nobody talks enough about what teaming actually does in year one.

I had a client — IT services firm, solid commercial revenue, zero federal footprint. They got their GSA Schedule approved, listed their services, and waited. Months went by. No awards. They had the credential but no federal relationships, no past performance, and no idea where to start.

Here's what changed it: we connected them with an established prime contractor pursuing a Department of Homeland Security IT opportunity. Our client's exact capabilities filled a gap the prime needed to cover. They came in as a subcontractor — their first federal project, real past performance now on record, and a whole new vocabulary for how federal buyers think.

Twelve months later, they submitted their first prime bid. They won.

Teaming isn't a shortcut. It's the protocol most small businesses skip because nobody explains it clearly. The GSA Schedule gives you the vehicle. Teaming gives you the launch ramp — and the past performance that makes the next bid easier to win.

If you have a Schedule and haven't mapped your sub-prime options yet — that's exactly where I'd start.
