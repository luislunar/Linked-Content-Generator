Wrong question: "What's the lowest price I can offer on GSA?"

Right question: "What's the highest price I can defend?"

Most small businesses walk into GSA negotiations trying to shave margins. That backwards instinct quietly kills competitiveness for years.

Five pricing mistakes I see constantly:

1. **No headroom built in** — GSA contracting officers negotiate down from your commercial price. If there's no room, you're at an unsustainable rate before the contract even starts.

2. **Ignoring the Price Reductions Clause** — If a preferred commercial customer gets a better deal and you don't update your GSA price, you're in violation. This catches companies completely off guard.

3. **Pricing too low to win** — Counterintuitive but true: pricing too cheap signals inexperience, makes delivery unprofitable, and flags your rates to auditors.

4. **Wrong labor category mapping** — Matching your services to incorrect SINs means your pricing gets compared against companies doing entirely different work.

5. **No Economic Price Adjustment clause** — Without escalation provisions, you're locked into today's rates when costs rise. Inflation doesn't pause for GovCon.

The Schedule isn't a price war. It's a pricing floor worth getting right the first time.
