Nobody talks enough about the cost of waiting.

I've had this conversation hundreds of times. A business owner tells me they want a GSA Schedule but they're not ready yet. They need more revenue. More commercial clients. More proof that the business can handle federal work. So they wait.

Three years later, I hear from them again. Same business, same hesitation — only now they've watched competitors win federal contracts they should have been competing for. The wait didn't build readiness. It just built regret.

One client finally came to us after three years of "almost." Good commercial revenue, strong services, every qualification in place. Nine months later, their Schedule was approved. Eleven months after that, their first federal award landed. And the thing they said to me? "I didn't know the Schedule *was* the way to build federal past performance. I thought I needed it first."

That's the trap. The GSA Schedule isn't the finish line you reach when you're established. It's the starting block for federal work. You don't earn your way in — you apply, get approved, and do the work once the door is open.

The best time to pursue a GSA Schedule is when your commercial business is stable and you're ready to diversify. Not when you feel fully prepared for federal. That day doesn't come without the credential in hand.

The government market doesn't wait for you to feel ready.

If you're being honest with yourself — what's the real reason you haven't started the GSA process yet?
