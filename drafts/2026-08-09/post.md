Nobody talks about the real reason most teaming agreements go nowhere.

It's not bad timing. It's not a bad prime. It's not the market.

It's that small businesses show up to the teaming conversation with nothing concrete to offer.

I've seen this a hundred times. A company reaches out to a prime, introduces themselves, and says some version of: "We'd love to be on your team. We have capacity and we do great work."

The prime nods, takes the card, and never calls back.

Here's why: primes aren't looking for bandwidth. They're looking for a specific NAICS code they don't hold. A socioeconomic certification that unlocks a set-aside they can't compete on alone. A cleared facility in a region where they don't have coverage. A past performance in a specific agency vertical that fills a gap on their capability statement.

Before you reach out to a prime, get specific. What do you bring that they cannot get elsewhere? What gap on their team do you fill that has a dollar amount attached to it?

If you can't answer that in one sentence, you're not ready to team. And no amount of follow-up emails will fix a pitch that never had a hook.

Teaming is a business development strategy. Treat it like one.
