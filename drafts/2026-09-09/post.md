Nobody talks enough about what's actually sitting in Sources Sought notices.

I worked with a small professional services firm last year — 12 people, solid track record, clients they were proud of. They'd had their GSA Schedule for eight months and expected the phone to ring. It didn't.

When they called us, we asked one question: how many Sources Sought notices had they responded to in the past eight months? Zero. They didn't even know Sources Sought notices existed as a pipeline tool. They thought their job was to wait for RFQs to drop and respond when they showed up.

We spent two weeks showing them how to monitor beta.SAM.gov, how to respond to Sources Sought notices before a solicitation ever opens, and how to position as a potential prime — not just a sub waiting for scraps.

Three months later: two RFQs directly connected to notices they had responded to. Not awards yet. But real conversations with real CO interest.

The GSA Schedule opened the door. Reading the market opened the revenue.

If you have a Schedule and haven't responded to a Sources Sought notice this month — when did you last search beta.SAM.gov by your NAICS code and actually look at what's there?
