$21.9 billion.

That's what the Department of Defense alone paid to small businesses in Q2 2026.

Not the full fiscal year. One quarter. One agency.

I get the same objection on almost every evaluation call I take:

"The federal market isn't built for companies our size."

The data doesn't agree.

→ DoD: $21.9B to small businesses (Q2 2026)
→ Department of Veterans Affairs: $3.6B
→ Department of Homeland Security: $3.2B

That's $28.7B from three agencies in a single quarter. And they're not done spending.

The market isn't closed to small businesses. The access layer is.

Companies that captured a piece of that $28.7B weren't bigger or better-capitalized. They were positioned: SAM.gov active, a vehicle in place, pricing that held up under a contracting officer's review.

The objection I hear — "we're too small for this" — is almost never the actual barrier. The real reasons are usually: no vehicle, a lapsed registration, or pricing that's never been pressure-tested against what a CO expects to see.

The money is moving. The question is whether your company is visible to the people authorizing it.

What's the one thing actually standing between you and your first federal award?
