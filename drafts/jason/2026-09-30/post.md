"We don't have federal past performance."

I hear some version of this on almost every discovery call I take. And every time, my answer is the same: that's not the wall you think it is.

The federal market has a specific mechanism built for exactly this situation — it's called teaming. When your company subs to an established prime, the federal government credits you for that work. Contracting officers record it. Agencies see it. Inside 12–18 months, you have a federal track record.

I talked to an IT firm last quarter. Eight years of commercial work. Zero federal history. The past-performance objection came up in minute three. Four months later, they had a teaming agreement with a mid-tier prime that needed their specific stack and were in active conversations with two agencies.

The concern didn't disappear. It became a non-issue.

What keeps most companies on the sidelines isn't the past-performance requirement — it's the assumption that the requirement is impossible to meet without already having met it. That's circular logic. And it costs small businesses 12–18 months of market time they don't get back.

The past-performance wall isn't as solid as it looks.
