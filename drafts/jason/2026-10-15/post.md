"Having a certification means the contracts will follow."

That's the objection I hear most on calls — from SDVOSB and 8(a) companies who applied for a GSA Schedule three years ago and haven't seen a federal dollar yet.

Certifications are doors. You still have to knock — and knock on the right ones.

→ **8(a):** Sole-source authority up to $4.5M ($7M for manufacturing). Real ceiling — but only reaches it with active agency relationships and sources-sought tracking.

→ **SDVOSB:** VA set-asides are serious money — $5.7B to small businesses last quarter alone. Most veterans get certified, update their SAM profile, and stop there. That's not a strategy.

→ **HUBZone:** A 10% price evaluation preference in competitive bids. Useful when the margin is close — not a guarantee when your technical score is weak.

→ **WOSB / EDWOSB:** Set-asides exist only in eligible NAICS codes. Half the companies I talk to haven't checked if their primary code is even on the list.

→ **Any cert + GSA Schedule:** Now you have a vehicle. Without one, the cert is a credential with nowhere to go.

The certification gets you on the field. The pipeline work is what actually scores.

Which cert do you have — and has it generated a single real contract for your business yet?
