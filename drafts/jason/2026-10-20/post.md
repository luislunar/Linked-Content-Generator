Teaming is sold as the shortcut into federal.

Most of the time, it's not.

Here's what I actually see on calls every week:

→ Small businesses team up to avoid the GSA paperwork. Three years later, they've done four sub awards and have zero past performance in their own name.
→ The prime controls the agency relationship. You're a line item on their proposal, not a partner.
→ Your margins get squeezed twice — once by the prime, once by your underlying GSA pricing.
→ When the prime loses the recompete, your pipeline disappears overnight.

Teaming has two legitimate uses: you genuinely can't self-perform the full scope, or you're using it to build a track record on a specific NAICS before you compete solo. That's it.

If you're teaming because the Schedule application looks complicated, that's a readiness problem — not a strategy.

The companies I see move fastest don't wait to be a sub. They get their own vehicle and compete directly. Teaming can be a step. It should never be the plan.

Have you used teaming to get a foot in the door? Did it build something lasting, or did you end up stuck in the sub lane?
