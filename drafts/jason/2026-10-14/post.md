Wrong question: "What price should I put on my GSA Schedule?"

Right question: "What happens when a CO opens GSA Advantage and benchmarks my rate against four competitors?"

I talk to small businesses every week who treat GSA pricing like a bid number — go low, win work.

Here's what actually happens:

→ The CO pulls up GSA Advantage. Your $85/hour IT rate sits next to competitors at $110–$140.
→ You look desperate, not competitive. In federal procurement, "cheap" raises flags, not interest.
→ And your Schedule rate is your ceiling for the next 5 years. Lock in a bad number today and you need a formal modification to fix it.

The deals I see fall apart aren't from companies priced too high. They're from companies that gutted their margins trying to win on price — and then can't sustain the work when contracts do land.

GSA isn't eBay. Best value beats lowest cost. A price that signals capacity wins more work than one that signals desperation.
