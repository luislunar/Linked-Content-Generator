The Schedule application isn't where most companies lose. It's the pricing negotiation.

I had a call last month with an IT consultant — solid book of commercial business, $4M in annual revenue. They wanted the GSA Schedule to open federal doors. Everything looked right on paper.

Then I asked about their Basis of Award.

Blank stare.

The Basis of Award is the commercial transaction GSA benchmarks your Schedule pricing against. Submit the wrong one — an expired contract, an outlier discount, an inconsistent rate — and GSA has grounds to negotiate you into uncompetitive territory before you've won a single order.

Here's what happened:

→ The company cited a one-off project rate that undercut their standard pricing by 18%.

→ GSA asked for a price concession from that number. They had no room.

→ 11 weeks of back-and-forth. Final Schedule rate: 14% below their normal commercial price.

They got approved. But their Schedule makes them uncompetitive against companies who actually did the pricing prep.

The fix is simple: before you submit, find your most favorable commercial transaction — the one with the strongest margins. Document it properly. That's your Basis of Award anchor.

GSA will still negotiate. But they'll negotiate down from your ceiling, not your floor.
