"You need certifications to compete in the federal market."

I hear this on evaluation calls every week. It's only half right.

You need the *right* certification for the *right* customer set. The rest is a nice framed certificate and a lot of paperwork.

Here's how I actually rank them based on what I see move pipeline:

**HUBZone**
→ Highest ceiling if you qualify. Sole-source authority up to $4M for supplies, $7M for services. The hard part is the 35% employee residency requirement — most businesses can't meet it. The ones who do dominate their local market with almost no competition.

**8(a)**
→ Nine-year runway. Sole-source access at the agency's discretion. A built-in protected lane while you build past performance. The application is the difficult part — not the program itself.

**SDVOSB**
→ Veteran-owned set-asides. The VA alone awarded $5.7B to small businesses last quarter (Source: USAspending.gov). If eligibility applies to you, this is a direct key to one of the most active set-aside markets in the federal government.

**WOSB / EDWOSB**
→ Active across 83 NAICS codes, strongest in professional services. EDWOSB opens an additional tier of set-asides if you meet the economic disadvantage threshold. Real competitive edge — thinner if your target agencies don't consistently use set-asides.

None of them guarantee an award. All of them put you in a lane a competitor without the cert simply cannot enter.

The certification that matters is the one your target agency actually uses.
