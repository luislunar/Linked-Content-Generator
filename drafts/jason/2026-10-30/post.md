"My price has to be the lowest to win."

Wrong question. Here's the right one.

Every week I talk to businesses comparing their GSA Schedule price to a competitor's. They're focused on one thing: *how do I go lower?*

What they should be asking instead: *what can I actually defend to a Contracting Officer?*

GSA pricing isn't a race to the bottom. It works differently:

→ Your Schedule price is a ceiling — agencies negotiate below it at the task order level
→ Pricing yourself too low up front locks you in — raising it later requires a contract modification
→ A CO who sees "suspiciously low" gets cautious, not excited
→ The contractors who win consistently aren't the cheapest — they're the most defensible

The objection I hear most on calls: "I just need to get my foot in the door."

Getting in at a price you can't sustain isn't a strategy. It's a problem you're scheduling for year two.
