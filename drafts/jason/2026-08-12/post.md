"I need to undercut my commercial rate to win federal work."

That's the #1 pricing myth I hear this month.

**Wrong assumption:**
Your GSA rate needs to be lower than what you charge commercial clients.

**What's actually true:**
Your GSA rate is a ceiling — the maximum you're allowed to charge per task order. Individual agencies negotiate lower from there. Whether the number is "competitive" is the wrong thing to optimize for.

The three pricing levers that actually matter:

→ **Basis of Award:** GSA benchmarks your proposed rate against your best commercial customer. That customer's price becomes your floor. The negotiation starts there — not from a number you invented.

→ **Price Reductions Clause:** Give any similar commercial customer a lower rate after GSA approval? You're required to report it. Most small businesses don't know this clause exists until someone audits them.

→ **Reasonableness over cheapness:** COs evaluate whether your rate is *justified* — backed by market research and comparables. That's a different question than "are you the cheapest vendor on this SIN?"

Companies that consistently win federal work aren't pricing below commercial. They're making their rate defensible.

That's a different problem than "how low do I go."
