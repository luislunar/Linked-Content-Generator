Most GSA proposals I review have the same problem.

Not the service description. Not the past performance. The pricing.

Four mistakes I see on nearly every submission:

→ **No commercial invoice trail.** Your Basis of Award needs documentation. "We charge this commercially" isn't enough — a CO needs the paper to validate it.

→ **Pricing above your commercial rate.** GSA pricing must be at or below what you charge your best commercial customers. If it's not, expect a rejection or a renegotiation that guts your margins.

→ **Missing the Industrial Funding Fee.** The IFF is 0.75% of Schedule revenue. Most small businesses price like it doesn't exist, then lose money on every federal sale.

→ **Pricing from last year's cost structure.** Your GSA Schedule lasts 5 years. Price today's services at today's costs — not what your P&L looked like 18 months ago.

If your pricing doesn't hold up on paper, nothing else in that proposal matters.
