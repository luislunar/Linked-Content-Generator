"Your GSA pricing strategy might be working against you."

Here's what I hear on almost every intro call from a company that's tried to build their schedule themselves:

"We priced low to stay competitive."

That's the instinct. It's also the problem.

GSA pricing isn't a bid. It's a negotiation against your own commercial history. Basis of Award requires you to give the government pricing that's at least as good as your most favored customer — not your lowest price to anyone.

When you undercut your commercial rates to "look cheap," two things happen:

→ You anchor your approved schedule rates too low to cover costs on actual task orders.
→ You create a paper trail that limits your ability to negotiate up during modification.

The companies we work with that price correctly from the start protect their margins. The ones that don't spend the next year trying to fix it through modifications.

Your schedule is only as good as the rates you locked in on day one.
