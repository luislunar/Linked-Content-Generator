Before you finalize your GSA Schedule rates, read this.

Four pricing mistakes I see in almost every proposal I review — and every one of them costs small businesses margin they'll never get back.

→ Quoting your commercial invoice price as your GSA rate
GSA requires Most Favored Customer pricing. If any commercial client pays you less than what you're proposing on the Schedule, contracting officers will find it during negotiations. Start with your actual best commercial price, not your standard invoice.

→ Forgetting the 0.75% Industrial Funding Fee
The IFF comes off every GSA award automatically — it's how GSA funds the program. If you didn't build that fee into your Schedule rates, it comes straight out of your margin. That's not a rounding error on a multi-year contract.

→ Locking in rates with no escalation room
Your rates are set at award. Without a solid Economic Price Adjustment clause, inflation quietly eats the gap between your cost and your Schedule price over 5 years. I've seen companies locked into rates that made them uncompetitive by year 3.

→ Bundling all labor under one category
Contracting officers shop by CLIN. If you blend a senior consultant and a project coordinator under a single rate, you'll lose every RFQ where the CO needs the lower-cost option. Separate your labor categories. It takes more work upfront; it pays off every time.

None of these are obscure regulations. They're just easy to miss when you're filling out 100+ pages of paperwork for the first time without someone who's seen it before.
