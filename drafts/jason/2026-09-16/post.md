Last month I had a call with a twelve-person IT services firm.

Before I could finish my intro, the owner said it: "Federal contracts are for the Boeings and Leidos of the world."

I hear some version of that every single week.

Here's what I pulled up before we hung up: in Q2 2026 alone, small businesses collected $18.6 billion from the federal government — across just the top eight industry categories.

Not per year. One quarter.

He went quiet for a few seconds. That's what a busted belief actually sounds like.

Agencies are required by law to direct a portion of contracts to small businesses. That requirement isn't a courtesy — it's baked into the procurement process. The market is built for companies your size. Most just don't have the paperwork to get in.

Here's where he stood six months ago — and where he is now:

Before:
→ No federal revenue line
→ Commercial-only pipeline
→ Convinced the door was closed

After:
→ GSA Schedule approved
→ First RFQ in month seven
→ A federal pipeline he actually controls

The barrier was never his size — just the paperwork and the assumption that this market wasn't built for companies like his.

Source: USAspending.gov · Q2 2026, small business awards
