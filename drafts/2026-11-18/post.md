Before you spend another week refreshing SAM.gov, there are 5 better places to build federal pipeline.

SAM.gov is where contracts live after the relationship is already built. If that's your only source, you're competing cold — against vendors who've been in the room for months.

Here's where to look first:

1. Industry Days — agencies post these 30–90 days before the RFP drops. Attending puts your name in front of the CO before the competition even knows a contract is coming.

2. Sources Sought Notices — these aren't contracts. They're market research. Responding with a capability statement plants your company in the CO's memory and can shape how the final requirements get written.

3. USAspending.gov — search by NAICS code and agency to see who bought what in the last 12 months. The recompete is often 18–24 months from the original award date. Tracking incumbents is a full pipeline strategy on its own.

4. Agency Procurement Forecasts — most large agencies publish an annual forecast of upcoming contracts by category, value, and expected date. Find yours on the agency's small business office page. Almost no one uses it.

5. Small Business Outreach Events — every major agency runs them. These aren't generic networking. They're CO access points. The vendors who show up consistently get remembered when sources sought go out.

SAM.gov is the last step of a process that started months earlier.
