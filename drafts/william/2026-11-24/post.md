A question I hear on almost every call when someone is evaluating a GSA Schedule:

"Do I have to give the government my lowest price?"

It makes sense why they ask. The assumption is that the federal government gets some kind of permanent discount — that they're negotiating you down to your floor.

That's not how it works.

Your GSA price is based on your Basis of Award — the rate you give your most-favored commercial customer. Not your lowest one-off. Not what you'd charge a price-sensitive client on a bad quarter. The commercial rate you'd put in a preferred customer contract.

Here's where most people hurt themselves:

→ They assume "competitive" means cheapest and quote rates too low to sustain.
→ They don't realize that locked-in GSA rates follow you through the life of the contract.
→ They skip the negotiation — GSA's first offer is rarely their best.
→ They forget that wrong pricing today affects every RFQ response for years.

One business owner I talked to last month had priced his services at 20% below what he'd charge a comparable commercial client. He thought he was being competitive. He'd have been locking himself into rates that undercut his own margins for the next five years.

The Schedule is a long game. Pricing mistakes made at approval don't go away easily.
