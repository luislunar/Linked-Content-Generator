A question came up on a call last week that I hear more often than most people would expect.

"William, we did everything right. We got registered in SAM.gov almost two years ago. So why is our contracting officer telling us they can't process this award?"

They had done everything right — two years ago.

Here's what they didn't know: SAM.gov registration expires every 12 months. Theirs had quietly lapsed four months earlier. The auto-renewal reminder email had gone to the wrong inbox. Nobody caught it.

The award went to another vendor.

SAM.gov isn't a one-time setup. It's an active credential. Registration needs annual renewal. NAICS codes need to reflect what your business actually sells today — not just what you offered when you first registered. Entity information like your business address, banking details, and key officers has to stay current, because federal buyers can't pay a vendor whose record doesn't match.

Once they renewed the registration, corrected two NAICS codes that no longer matched their service mix, and updated their entity profile, they were cleared within the month.

Nothing about their actual capabilities had changed. The credential just needed to be current.

If you have federal opportunities in your pipeline right now — when did you last log into SAM.gov and verify everything's still active?
