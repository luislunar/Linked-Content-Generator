I had a call last week with a construction company owner — $4M in commercial revenue, solid track record, eight years in business.

He wanted to explore the GSA Schedule. But before I could even walk him through the basics, he stopped me:

"William, I'm worried my rates are just too high for the government to take seriously."

I hear some version of this on almost every call I take with commercial contractors. The assumption is that federal work means competing on price — and that anything above a mid-market rate will get passed over instantly.

Here's what I actually told him.

GSA pricing isn't about being the cheapest option in the room. It's about being defensible. The government wants to see that what you charge on your Schedule is reasonably consistent with what you charge your most favorable commercial customer. That's it. You document your commercial baseline, support your rates with invoices, and the negotiation becomes a paperwork exercise — not a race to the bottom.

Then I pulled up his NAICS code while we were still on the phone. Construction companies his size captured $3.7B in Q3 federal awards alone.

*Source · USAspending.gov*

His rates weren't the obstacle. His belief about his rates was.

We ended the call with a plan: pull his commercial invoices, build a clear pricing narrative, and document his most-favorable price to a commercial client. That's where it starts — not with lowering anything.
