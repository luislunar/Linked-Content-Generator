I had two calls in the same week last month.

Both owners ran IT consulting firms. Both got their GSA Schedule approved around the same time — about 18 months before I spoke to them. Similar size, similar NAICS codes, similar commercial track record.

One was frustrated he hadn't won a single federal contract.

The other had just closed his third.

Here's what separated them:

→ **Sources Sought responses.** The winner was responding to every relevant Sources Sought notice he could find — not pitching, just submitting a tight capability statement so the agency knew he existed before the RFQ dropped. The other owner didn't know that was an option.

→ **Industry days.** The winner was showing up to virtual industry days 6–8 months before solicitations went live. He was building familiarity. The other owner was waiting for a link to land in his inbox.

→ **Agency touchpoints.** The winner had identified two or three contracting offices at his target agencies and maintained a simple, consistent presence — emails, capability briefs, nothing aggressive. He wasn't invisible when the RFQ came out.

The Schedule opens the door. Pipeline strategy decides whether you walk through it.

A lot of small businesses get the credential and then wait. The ones landing contracts are treating every Sources Sought notice like an early preview — and showing up accordingly.

What's one thing you do to stay visible to agencies before a solicitation even goes live?
