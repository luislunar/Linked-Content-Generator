"Does my company even qualify?"

It's the first question on almost every call I take. Sometimes it's the opener. Sometimes it sits there for ten minutes while the owner explains their business — and then finally comes out.

I understand why. The GSA Schedule looks enormous from the outside: hundreds of pages, federal compliance language, a process that can run 6 to 12 months. It's natural to assume a company has to be a certain size before it's even worth asking.

Here's what I actually walk through on that call:

→ Two years in business. That's the baseline commercial track record GSA is looking for — not ten years, not a federal track record, just two.

→ Documented commercial revenue. The Schedule isn't reserved for large companies. You need to show you're selling something real, at commercial prices, to real customers.

→ No active federal tax liens or unresolved legal judgments. Clean record — a requirement before the application even starts.

→ Services or products that map to a GSA Special Item Number. There are hundreds of SINs across IT, construction, consulting, professional services, facilities, and more. Most businesses land somewhere.

→ An active SAM.gov registration. If it's lapsed or was never set up, that gets corrected first.

Most owners who call me genuinely worried about qualifying? They qualify.

The process is real work. But the starting line is a lot closer than most small business owners expect.
