This draft tests whether a first-call framing ("a question I hear on almost every first call") paired with a SAM.gov checklist can activate two distinct comment cohorts simultaneously: Schedule-holders who haven't audited their record recently and will recognize one of the five items as a near-miss, and prospects who didn't know SAM required active maintenance at all. The question hook is designed to surface latent anxiety — most owners assume an "active" status means they're fully compliant — which should lift reactions (recognition) and comments (owners naming which item they'd overlooked). The checklist format with five short, individually-nameable items lowers skim-to-engage friction compared to a prose post on the same topic, making it easier for readers to pick one item and comment specifically rather than respond to the topic in general. Expected score gains: reactions (relatable SAM compliance anxiety) and comments (high specificity of CTA — "what's one thing you found or almost missed" targets a named memory, not a broad opinion).
