Before you spend months pursuing a certification, make sure you understand what each one actually does.

I hear a version of this question on almost every first call: "Which certification do I need before we get on the GSA Schedule?"

The short answer: you don't need any. The GSA Schedule is open to any qualified small business. But what certifications do for you once you're on it — that's the real conversation.

Here's what I walk people through:

→ **Certifications are accelerants, not prerequisites.** 8(a), WOSB, SDVOSB, HUBZone — each one opens a lane of set-aside competition on top of the Schedule. They don't replace it.

→ **8(a) is a program, not a badge.** It comes with a development track, reporting requirements, and an eight-year sunset. If you qualify, it's one of the most powerful tools in GovCon. But it's a commitment.

→ **WOSB and SDVOSB are NAICS-specific.** Set-aside competition only applies in the codes where those firms are underrepresented. Check the qualifying list before you assume it covers your work.

→ **HUBZone requires ongoing presence.** Your principal office and 35% of your employees need to stay in a designated zone — and those zone maps can redraw.

→ **There's no required order.** Pursuing your certification and your Schedule in parallel usually saves six months or more compared to running them back-to-back.

The certification question is a good one. The timing just matters more than most people realize.
