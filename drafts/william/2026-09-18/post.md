A construction business owner called me about two years into trying to break into federal work.

Good company. Real revenue. A team that knew what they were doing.

But every RFP he looked at required past performance with federal agencies — and he had none. So he kept losing before he ever really got started.

One thing I ask on almost every first call is whether someone has looked into teaming arrangements. He hadn't. Hadn't even heard the term used that way.

Here's what I walked him through:

→ You don't need to win a prime contract first to build past performance. You can enter as a subcontractor with a company that already has the agency relationships.

→ Teaming isn't a consolation prize. It's the strategy most successful small businesses use in their first 1–3 years of federal work.

→ The prime gets capacity and bandwidth. You get your first federal record. If the scope fits, it works for both sides.

→ That past performance you build as a sub? It counts. Contracting officers see it. Future bids get easier.

He found a prime in his space, put together a teaming agreement, and landed a subcontract. First federal invoice about eight months after our call.

He's bidding as a prime himself now. Still working with that original partner.

The first award doesn't have to be solo.

If you broke into federal work through teaming, what made you go that route — and would you do it the same way again?
