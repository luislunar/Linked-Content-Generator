Last week I got off a call with a small construction company owner.

He'd had his GSA Schedule for almost a year. Zero contracts.

When I asked what he'd done to build a pipeline, he said: "Honestly, I've been waiting."

That's the answer I hear more than I expected. Owners spend months getting through the application — the paperwork, the pricing audit, the back-and-forth with the contracting officer — and then treat the Schedule like a lottery ticket they already scratched. If nobody called, they figured it wasn't working.

But the Schedule is a vehicle. It doesn't drive itself.

Here's what shifted for this owner: he started responding to sources sought notices in his NAICS codes every week. He picked two agencies where construction awards were active and reached out directly to the contracting specialists there. He set up a USAspending.gov search for recompetes in his category — so he could see what was coming before it posted publicly.

Three months later, he got his first award.

The pipeline almost never builds by accident. The difference I keep seeing between owners who build one and owners who don't usually comes down to one thing: whether they're moving or still waiting.

Which part of building a pipeline feels most unclear right now — sources sought, agency outreach, or tracking recompetes before they post?
