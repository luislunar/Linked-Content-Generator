A question I hear on almost every first call:

"We're registered in SAM.gov — we've been for years. We're good, right?"

Usually. But not always.

I talked to a business owner last month who was ready to move forward on his GSA Schedule. The company checked every box: strong commercial revenue, the right NAICS codes, two and a half years in business. The call was going great.

At the end, I asked when he'd last looked at his SAM.gov registration. He said something like, "Oh, it's fine — we did it a couple years ago."

He wasn't trying to be dismissive. He just didn't know what I knew:

SAM.gov registrations expire every 12 months. And SAM.gov does not send you a reliable reminder when that's about to happen.

His had lapsed six months earlier. Quietly. No notification. If he'd submitted the GSA application that week, it would have been rejected before anyone even read the paperwork.

The fix was simple — renewal took two days. The application got back on track. Nothing permanently lost.

But here's what gets me: this comes up on more calls than I'd expect. Experienced business owners, some with federal history, who haven't logged into SAM since they first registered.

A lapsed SAM registration doesn't just block a GSA application. It freezes any outstanding federal payments on active contracts too.

Three steps, right now: log in, check your expiration date, set a calendar reminder 60 days out. It's free. It takes 10 minutes. And it protects everything else you're building toward.
