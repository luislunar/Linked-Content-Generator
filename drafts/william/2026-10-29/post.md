Before you decide certifications are the first step — a call I had a few months ago might reframe that.

A business owner called me looking to pursue federal work. Eight years in business, solid revenue, strong commercial track record. She'd been waiting on her 8(a) application before even looking at the GSA Schedule. Her reasoning made sense: certifications unlock set-aside contracts, so shouldn't she get that first?

Here's what I told her.

The GSA Schedule doesn't require any set-aside certification. 8(a), WOSB, SDVOSB, HUBZone — these open specific pools of work on top of the Schedule, but they're not prerequisites for getting on it.

What they actually are: multipliers.

Once her Schedule was approved, her 8(a) status would give her access to the 8(a) STARS III vehicle, sole-source awards under $4.5M, and a prioritized lane at every agency running 8(a) set-asides. The Schedule is the door. The certification is the key to another wing.

She applied nine months ago. Schedule approved last month. She's now working both tracks simultaneously.

Most owners I talk to think these are a sequence. They're actually a stack.

If you're holding a certification and haven't touched the GSA Schedule yet — or the reverse — you may be leaving an entire lane on the table.
