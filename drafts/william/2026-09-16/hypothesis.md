This draft tests whether a `truth` hook framing the post-approval compliance burden drives higher engagement among existing GSA Schedule holders — who may experience the anxiety of recognition ("I haven't checked mine in months") — and prospects evaluating the Schedule, who want realistic expectations before committing. The compliance/maintenance angle is underserved in GovCon content, which skews heavily toward acquisition and pipeline; a post about what happens *after* approval creates a distinct tension point that neither today's Josh post (pricing pitfalls) nor Jason's recent posts cover. Expected score movers: **reactions** from holders who feel seen (recognition effect) and **comments** from both confident holders sharing their routine and anxious ones asking what "checking status" actually means. The CTA ("when did you last log in?") is deliberately specific enough to surface self-disclosure rather than generic agreement, rewarding honest responses from two opposite experience levels — active maintainers (social proof) and neglectful holders (urgency signal).
