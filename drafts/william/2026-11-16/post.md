A question I hear on almost every call once we get to pricing:

"Should I just charge the government what I charge my commercial clients?"

It sounds like a fair starting point. It's usually the wrong one.

Here are five pricing realities most small businesses miss before they submit their GSA application:

→ **Basis of Award locks you in.** GSA will ask for your most favorable commercial price. The reference customer you name sets the floor you'll be held to throughout the life of the contract.

→ **The price reduction clause follows you.** Lower a commercial rate after your Schedule is approved? You have to report it to GSA. This one catches a lot of holders off guard a year or two in.

→ **Know your floor before the CO pushes back.** Contracting officers negotiate rates — that's expected. Where businesses get in trouble is not knowing how low they can actually go before the numbers stop working.

→ **Labor categories will get scrutinized.** LCAT descriptions that don't align with the actual work get flagged during negotiations, or worse, during an audit. The detail work matters upfront.

→ **A thin margin is a real problem post-award.** I've talked to holders who got approved, won their first task order, and realized their approved rate didn't cover their actual costs. Getting pricing right before submission matters more than getting to submission fast.

Pricing is one of those areas where doing it carefully at the start saves a lot of pain later.
