"I got approved eight months ago. Why haven't I won anything yet?"

I take that call more than almost any other.

And almost every time, it comes down to the same few things. The Schedule got approved — and then it got filed.

Here's what usually hasn't happened:

→ The GSA catalog was never updated to reflect current pricing or services
→ No modifications were submitted after the team or offerings changed
→ Nobody has been responding to RFQs on GSA Advantage or eBuy
→ SAM.gov registration lapsed somewhere in the gap year

A GSA Schedule is an access mechanism, not an automatic contract generator. Agencies can find you — but they still have to want to reach out, and when they do, someone has to be on the other end.

The business owners I see turning their Schedule into actual federal revenue have one thing in common: they treat it like an active sales channel, not a credential they earned once and filed away.

That means keeping the catalog current, knowing your NAICS codes well, responding to sources sought notices, and showing up consistently in the market.

Getting approved is real work. Staying active is a different kind of work — and most people don't have a plan for it going in.

If you got your Schedule in the last year and haven't had a maintenance check-in, that's usually where I'd start the conversation.

What's been the hardest part of staying active post-approval — or if you're still pre-approval, what's your plan for after the notice arrives?
