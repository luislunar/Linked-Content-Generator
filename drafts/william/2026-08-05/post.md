The question I get on almost every first call isn't about pricing or past performance.

It's this: *"Before we go any further — can you just explain what a GSA Schedule actually is?"*

I love that question. It tells me the person is thinking it through, not just collecting brochures.

Here's what I tell them:

A GSA Schedule isn't a contract. It's a pre-approval. The federal government vets your company once — your pricing, your commercial work history, your financials — and then lists you as an approved vendor that any federal agency in the country can hire from directly. No new competitive bid every time. Just a purchase order against your existing Schedule.

Once you're on it, a federal buyer can find your company and purchase without going through a months-long procurement process. You become a known option in a market that never really closes.

Three things that typically determine eligibility:

→ Two years in business with tax returns to back it up
→ Commercial clients you can document — the government wants to see real work history
→ A NAICS code that maps to something agencies actually buy

The hard part isn't qualifying. Most solid commercial businesses already meet those basics.

The hard part is the 100+ hours of paperwork — pricing exhibits, compliance documentation, negotiations with the contracting officer. That's where most owners give up, and honestly, it's where I spend most of my days.
