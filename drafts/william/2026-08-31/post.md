Last month, I got a call from a small IT firm owner.

She'd had her GSA Schedule for three years. She'd used it maybe twice.

She called because she'd just received a modification notice from her Contracting Officer and didn't understand what was being asked of her.

When I pulled her record up with her, we found:

→ Her rates hadn't changed since day one — and her commercial pricing had climbed 15% since she applied
→ One listed service no longer matched the work she was actually doing
→ Her business address was two years out of date

None of it was intentional. She'd treated the Schedule like a finish line, not a foundation.

We filed the modifications, updated the rates, corrected the scope.

Six weeks later, she won her first federal task order.

A GSA Schedule is a living contract. Rates drift. Scope shifts. Life changes. The owners who keep it current are the ones who can actually compete when the right opportunity shows up.

If you've had your Schedule for more than a year and haven't reviewed it — that's worth a look this week.

What's the part of Schedule maintenance nobody warned you about when you first got approved?
