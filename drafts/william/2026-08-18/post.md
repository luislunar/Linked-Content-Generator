A question I get more than you'd think on first calls:

"I have my WOSB certification. Does that actually help me get federal contracts?"

Short answer: yes — more than most people realize.

Last month I talked to a woman who runs an IT consulting firm out of Atlanta. Seven years in business. Strong commercial revenue. Her WOSB certification had been sitting in a folder she'd never revisited — she assumed it was more of a formality than a real market advantage.

Here's what she didn't know:

→ Federal agencies have specific set-aside goals — they're required by law to award a percentage of contracts exclusively to women-owned small businesses
→ WOSB set-asides shrink your competition from thousands of vendors to a much smaller pool
→ Many contracting officers actively scan for WOSB holders when building their vendor shortlists

She had a real advantage in hand. She just didn't know which door it opened.

What surprised her most wasn't learning the opportunity existed — it was learning she'd already done the hardest part.

This is true for SDVOSB holders, HUBZone businesses, and 8(a) participants too. The certification isn't just a label. It's a filter that puts you in front of buyers who are specifically looking for businesses like yours.

If you have a certification that's been sitting unused, I'm genuinely curious:

What's been the biggest barrier to connecting it to federal opportunities?
