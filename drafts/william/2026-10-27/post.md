Nobody warns small business owners about the beliefs that keep them from the federal market for years.

I hear the same ones on almost every first call.

→ "We don't have federal past performance — so we can't qualify."
The Schedule doesn't require a government contract on record. Commercial revenue and a solid two-year financial history are the real baseline.

→ "Once we're approved, contracts will start flowing."
The Schedule is a purchasing vehicle, not a sales guarantee. It opens the door to $800B+ in federal spending. You still have to knock.

→ "We're too small for this market."
The MAS program sets aside specific contract pools for small businesses. Being small isn't a barrier here — it's often an advantage.

→ "It takes years to get through the application."
Typical timeline is 6–12 months. The delays I see most aren't from GSA — they come from incomplete submissions on the application side.

→ "SAM.gov registration and the GSA Schedule are the same thing."
SAM is the vendor database every federal buyer uses. The Schedule is a separate contract vehicle you apply for. Both matter. They're not interchangeable.

Our team walks through all of these on first calls every single day. The businesses that move fastest are almost always the ones who called before they thought they were ready.
