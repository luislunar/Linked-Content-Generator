I got on a call recently with a business owner who'd had their GSA Schedule for about eighteen months.

Zero pipeline movement. Not even a sources sought response on record.

The week before, I'd spoken with someone in the same industry, similar size, Schedule for ten months. Three active opportunities being tracked — one heading toward a quote.

Same tool. Very different picture.

I've been paying attention to what separates them. Here's what the second group consistently does:

→ **They respond to sources sought notices.** Most businesses skip them — "just a pre-solicitation, nothing to quote on yet." The ones building pipeline treat sources sought like a first conversation with the agency. They introduce their capability, ask smart questions, and are a known quantity before the RFQ ever posts.

→ **They show up before the opportunity opens.** Industry days, agency open houses, a quick capability statement intro to a program office. Contracting officers remember names — and that matters when competition starts.

→ **They work recompetes strategically.** When an incumbent hasn't delivered, the agency wants options. A recompete with a struggling contractor is a very different math than starting fresh — and the businesses who understand that prioritize those accordingly.

→ **They've set NAICS code alerts on SAM.gov.** They know what's moving in their space before their competitors notice. That extra week on a sources sought notice is often the difference between being in the conversation and showing up late.

The Schedule gets you into the market. Your pipeline habits are what decide what happens next.
