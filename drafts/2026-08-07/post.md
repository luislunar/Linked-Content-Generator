I talked to a small business owner last year who'd had her GSA Schedule active for 18 months — and had never earned a single dollar of federal revenue.

Not because the Schedule didn't work. Because she'd filed the paperwork, celebrated the approval, and stopped. Her catalog sat untouched on GSA Advantage. She assumed the contracts would come to her.

They don't.

We shifted her approach. The first three months, SAM.gov became her daily read — not headlines, but Sources Sought notices, presolicitation announcements, and agency procurement history. She identified three recurring needs that aligned with her NAICS codes exactly. The next three months, she attended two virtual industry days: one for a VA contracting office, one for a DHS event. She introduced herself to a prime contractor who needed a certified sub on an upcoming recompete. They signed a teaming agreement within the week. Month seven: first award. Month nine: a second — a direct award through the GSA Schedule, triggered by a Contracting Officer who found her profile on GSA Advantage after she finally cleaned up her catalog.

The Schedule is not a passive vehicle. It's a door. You still have to knock.
