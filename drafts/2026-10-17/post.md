Nobody warns you that getting the Schedule is step one — not the destination.

I took a call last year. Owner had been active on the GSA MAS for 14 months. Prices negotiated. SAM.gov current. Every checkbox checked. Zero awards. She was ready to walk away from the whole thing.

I asked her one question: "What does your pipeline work look like week to week?"

Silence.

She'd been waiting. Assuming agencies would search the catalog, find her company, and reach out. That's not how federal procurement works. Contracting officers don't browse. They award to companies they already know — from industry days, sources sought responses, names that showed up when market research started six months before the solicitation dropped.

We spent 30 minutes together. Pulled three recompetes hitting her NAICS codes in the next 90 days. Found a DHS industry day she could register for that same week. Identified four active sources sought notices where she could put her company's name in front of COs before any RFQ ever existed.

Three months later, she submitted her first bid response.

The Schedule gets you to the table. Pipeline work is what keeps you in the room.

If you have an active GSA Schedule right now — how intentionally are you working pipeline? Weekly? Monthly? Or are you still waiting for the phone to ring?
