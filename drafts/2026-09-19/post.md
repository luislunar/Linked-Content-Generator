A veteran-owned security firm came to me two years into their federal pursuit with zero contracts to show for it.

They had their SDVOSB certification. Solid commercial past performance. A legitimate business. The problem? Every opportunity they found required a contract vehicle they didn't have. Contracting officers couldn't reach them through their preferred procurement channels. They were certified — and invisible.

We got them onto the GSA Schedule. Nine months to approval. One month after that, they responded to their first set-aside RFQ — reserved specifically for service-disabled veteran-owned businesses — and won.

The certification alone wasn't the unlock. The Schedule was.

I've seen this pattern dozens of times. Your 8(a), SDVOSB, WOSB, or HUBZone cert can cut your competition dramatically inside a set-aside — sometimes from hundreds of vendors to a handful. But that only applies if agencies can reach you through the vehicle they're using to procure. Most federal buying runs through established contract vehicles. The cert doesn't get you on those. You have to get there separately, then the cert does its job.

Certified but not contracted? You're holding a key to a door you haven't opened yet.

What certification does your business hold — and do you have a contract vehicle connected to it?
